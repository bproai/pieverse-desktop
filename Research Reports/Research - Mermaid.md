# Mermaid Chart – Company Report

This venture studio support provided initial funding (undisclosed) and paired founder Knut Sveidqvist with CEO Andrew Firestone to build a company around Mermaid’s technology ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=Markdown,Andrew%20Firestone%20as%20his%20CEO)) ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CMatching%20the%20open%20source%20software,me%20coming%20in%2C%E2%80%9D%20Firestone%20explained)).

## Financial Projections and Monetization

Mermaid Chart is still in an early commercial stage, and **financial projections** have not been publicly released. As a recently funded startup, the company’s focus has been on product development and user growth rather than immediate revenue figures. Nevertheless, some insight into its monetization strategy and growth expectations can be gathered from its business model and investor remarks:

- **Subscription SaaS Model:** Mermaid Chart employs a classic freemium SaaS model built on top of the open-source tool. The core diagramming platform is accessible with basic features for free, while advanced capabilities are part of paid plans. The **Pro tier** is priced around **$6.67 per user per month (≈$80 annually)** ([Pricing | Mermaid Chart](https://www.mermaidchart.com/pricing#:~:text=Choose%20the%20right%20plan%20for,annually%3A%20%2480%20%2F%20team)), targeting professional and enterprise teams. - **User Base and Conversion Potential:** The Mermaid open-source project has a massive user base, which forms the top of Mermaid Chart’s funnel. The company’s strategy is to convert a portion of these enthusiasts into paying customers by offering value-added services (hosting, editors, collaboration, AI, support). Even a small conversion of the millions of users into paid seats represents significant revenue potential. CEO Andrew Firestone noted that *“millions of non-technical users”* in businesses need diagramming and flowchart tools ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=Firestone%20added%20that%20millions%20of,still%20quite%20a%20ways%20off)), suggesting Mermaid Chart aims to expand usage beyond the developer community into broader enterprise teams. This expansive user targeting implies high growth expectations: investors are betting that Mermaid Chart can tap not only the existing developer user base but also a much larger market of knowledge workers who currently use alternatives like Visio or Lucidchart.

The company’s long-term vision (discussed below) to compete in **workflow and process automation** hints at ambitious revenue streams beyond just diagramming subscriptions.

## Product Evolution: From Open-Source Library to Enterprise Platform

Mermaid Chart’s product has undergone a notable evolution from its origins as an open-source library into a comprehensive commercial diagramming platform. The journey spans nearly a decade, with key milestones marking how the product and features have grown:

- **2015 – Origin of Mermaid (Open Source):** The Mermaid project was created by Knut Sveidqvist around 2015. Sveidqvist, then a software architect, conceived Mermaid after losing a Microsoft Visio file and wishing for a “better way” to create diagrams as text ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=There%E2%80%99s%20some%20irony%20in%20Microsoft,%E2%80%9D)) ([Mermaid Chart: How This Company Creates Complex Diagrams From Markdown-Style Code](https://pulse2.com/mermaid-chart-knut-sveidqvist-profile/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CThe%20inspiration%20for%20starting%20the,%E2%80%9D)). He started the Mermaid.js open-source library, naming it after the *“Little Mermaid”* movie that happened to be playing when he began coding ([Mermaid Chart: How This Company Creates Complex Diagrams From Markdown-Style Code](https://pulse2.com/mermaid-chart-knut-sveidqvist-profile/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CThis%20was%20eight%20years%20ago,%E2%80%99%E2%80%9D)). Mermaid.js introduced a **Markdown-like syntax** for defining diagrams (e.g., flowcharts, sequence diagrams, etc.) which a JavaScript engine would render into visuals. Early on, Mermaid focused on basic **flowcharts**, but over time the library expanded to support **many diagram types** – including UML class diagrams, state charts, user journey maps, Gantt charts, entity-relationship diagrams and more ([Include diagrams in your Markdown files with Mermaid - The GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/developer-skills/github/include-diagrams-markdown-files-mermaid/#:~:text=Mermaid%20is%20a%20JavaScript%20based,even%20the%20dreaded%20Gantt%20chart)). The open-source tool gained traction among developers for its simplicity and ability to be integrated in text documents.

- **2016–2021 – Community Growth:** Over the next several years, Mermaid.js flourished as a community-driven project. Contributors worldwide added features and diagram types, and Mermaid became a staple for developers needing quick diagrams in documentation. The project accumulated tens of thousands of stars on GitHub and millions of downloads. Critically, **Mermaid was natively integrated into major developer platforms** – for example, GitLab added Mermaid support, and in **February 2022 GitHub officially enabled Mermaid diagrams in Markdown files** ([Include diagrams in your Markdown files with Mermaid - The GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/developer-skills/github/include-diagrams-markdown-files-mermaid/#:~:text=Mermaid%20is%20a%20JavaScript%20based,even%20the%20dreaded%20Gantt%20chart)). This native support in GitHub and GitLab (widely used for code hosting and collaboration) massively increased Mermaid’s reach, making it almost a default tool for text-based diagrams in software documentation ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=syntax)). By 2023, Mermaid.js had achieved over **8 million users** according to its creator ([Mermaid Chart: How This Company Creates Complex Diagrams From Markdown-Style Code](https://pulse2.com/mermaid-chart-knut-sveidqvist-profile/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CI%E2%80%99m%20the%20creator%20of%20the,%E2%80%9D)), demonstrating its popularity as an open-source solution.

- **2022 – Company Formation (Mermaid Chart):** Despite Mermaid’s success, Sveidqvist had initially *“no plans for monetization”* ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=Mermaid%2C%20the%20open%20source%20diagramming,Andrew%20Firestone%20as%20his%20CEO)). That changed in 2022 when **Open Core Ventures (OCV)** identified Mermaid as a candidate for commercialization. OCV (led by GitLab’s CEO) specializes in helping open-source projects become companies ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=The%20OCV%20system%20is%20to,the%20investment%20quite%20a%20bit)). In 2022, OCV partnered with Sveidqvist to formally establish **Mermaid Chart** as a startup, with Sveidqvist as CTO and Andrew Firestone brought on as CEO ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=his%20own%20job%20as%20a,Andrew%20Firestone%20as%20his%20CEO)). During this period, the focus was on building **hosted services around the open-source core** ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=Now%20the%20company%2C%20which%20builds,a%20dozen%20more%20angel%20investors)) – essentially creating a cloud-based Mermaid platform with additional features that individual users and enterprises would pay for. Throughout its evolution, Mermaid Chart has emphasized it will continue supporting the open-source Mermaid.js project as well – some of the improvements made in the commercial product trickle back to the open source core, benefitting the community ([Announcements | Mermaid](https://mermaid.js.org/news/announcements.html#:~:text=Why%20It%20Matters%20for%20Mermaid,JS)). This open-core approach means the Mermaid library should continue to improve (with Mermaid Chart’s funding) while the company builds proprietary features on top.

In summary, **Mermaid Chart’s product evolution** can be seen as a progression from a **developer-centric text tool** to a **full-featured, AI-augmented diagramming platform**. Starting with a simple open-source library, it now offers a rich web application with **multiple editing modes (text & visual)**, **AI assistance**, a growing library of templates, and **enterprise-grade collaboration**. This evolution reflects Mermaid Chart’s goal of maintaining the power and precision of text-based diagramming (which existing Mermaid users love) while dramatically improving accessibility and integration for broader use cases. It’s a rare example of an open-source developer tool transforming into a user-friendly SaaS product without alienating its core tech-savvy user base.

## Market Position and Strategic Outlook

Mermaid Chart operates at the intersection of the **diagramming software market** and the **enterprise collaboration/tools market**. Its unique origins and approach give it a distinct position, but it also faces established competitors. Here’s an analysis of where Mermaid Chart stands and where it’s headed:

- **Competitive Landscape:** Traditionally, business diagramming and visual collaboration has been dominated by GUI-based tools. **Microsoft Visio** (part of Office) is a long-standing solution for flowcharts and diagrams, especially in corporate settings. In the last decade, web-based competitors have flourished: **Lucidchart** (by Lucid Software) offers online diagramming and has millions of users; **Miro** (and Mural) provide visual whiteboard platforms for brainstorming and flow diagrams; other players include **SmartDraw**, **Gliffy**, **Cacoo**, and open-source **draw.io/Diagrams.net**. Mermaid Chart enters this arena with a different spin – its foundation is text-based, appealing initially to engineers and technical users. It most directly competes with the likes of **PlantUML** and **GraphViz** (which also generate diagrams from code), and with **D2** (another text-based diagramming language startup). However, with the addition of its Visual Editor and user-friendly features, Mermaid Chart also competes with mainstream diagramming apps like Lucidchart or even Visio, since it now offers similar ease-of-use for creating common diagrams. 

  That said, Mermaid Chart’s leadership does not see direct *feature-for-feature* competition with tools like Lucidchart as its endgame. CEO Andrew Firestone noted that the company’s **vision is “significantly larger” and not about disrupting Lucidchart or Miro** on their own turf ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=Firestone%20added%20that%20millions%20of,still%20quite%20a%20ways%20off)). The plan is to leverage Mermaid’s unique strengths (text-based definitions, integration into code workflows, and now AI/automation) to tackle a bigger space. In Firestone’s words, Mermaid Chart wants to *“go after ServiceNow and similar workflow automation services”* in the long run ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=easier,still%20quite%20a%20ways%20off)). This is a telling statement: **ServiceNow** is a major enterprise platform for IT service management and workflow automation. Targeting that suggests Mermaid Chart is aiming to evolve from a diagramming tool into a **process automation and knowledge-management platform**. The rationale is that diagrams (especially text-defined ones) can become living documentation of business processes, which then tie into workflow execution. If Mermaid Chart can turn static diagrams into dynamic workflows, it could compete in the business process management (BPM) or automation market, setting it apart from pure diagramming tools.

- **Market Size and Opportunity:** The broader market for diagramming and visual collaboration tools is large and growing, fueled by digital transformation and distributed work. Millions of knowledge workers use diagrams to communicate ideas, map processes, and document systems. Lucidchart and Miro, for instance, have attained valuations in the billions of dollars, underscoring the market’s size. Mermaid Chart’s addressable market includes **software engineers, architects, product managers, business analysts, and any professional who needs to visualize processes or concepts**. According to one analysis, around *65% of people are visual learners* ([Mermaid Chart: How This Company Creates Complex Diagrams From Markdown-Style Code](https://pulse2.com/mermaid-chart-knut-sveidqvist-profile/#:~:text=Mermaid%20Chart%20is%20addressing%20a,%E2%80%9D)), and with over *62% of companies offering remote/flexible work as of 2023* ([Mermaid Chart: How This Company Creates Complex Diagrams From Markdown-Style Code](https://pulse2.com/mermaid-chart-knut-sveidqvist-profile/#:~:text=The%20Importance%20of%20Communication%20Tools,For%20Distributed%20Enterprise%20Teams)), the need for clear visual communication tools is higher than ever. Mermaid Chart is well-positioned to tap into this need: it already has millions of tech-savvy users from Mermaid.js, and by adding features for less technical users, it can capture a slice of the broader enterprise collaboration audience that might otherwise use Visio or a whiteboard tool.

  Moreover, Mermaid Chart’s open-source distribution gives it a **bottom-up adoption advantage**. Developers can introduce Mermaid diagrams into their projects freely (indeed many already do via GitHub, GitLab, etc.), which can organically spread within organizations. This bottom-up trend has been seen with other open-core companies (e.g., Draw.io became popular via Confluence/GitHub integrations). Mermaid Chart can leverage this to gain footholds in companies without the expensive sales efforts that pure enterprise software requires. Once teams are using the free Mermaid, upselling them to the paid Mermaid Chart service for collaboration, easier editing, and support becomes a natural progression.

- **Differentiation:** Mermaid Chart’s differentiation lies in its **blend of text-based and visual workflows**, its **AI features**, and its **open-core community backing**. No major competitor offers the ability to seamlessly switch between code and drag-and-drop editing – this is a unique selling point, especially for organizations that have both technical and non-technical team members collaborating. For example, a developer can quickly write out a complex architecture diagram in text, and a business analyst on the same team can later adjust that diagram using the visual editor without touching code. This dual-mode editing is a strong differentiator ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=As%20a%20text,the%20two%20interfaces%20at%20will)). Additionally, Mermaid Chart’s heavy investment in **AI for diagram generation and correction** is ahead of what most incumbents offer. While competitors like Miro have started adding AI assistance, Mermaid Chart’s integration (e.g. generating a complete diagram from a prompt) is particularly deep because of its textual DNA – it can leverage large language models to produce actual diagram code. This not only speeds up diagram creation but also lowers the barrier for anyone to use the tool (you can literally *ask* it for the diagram you want). 

  Being **open-source at the core** is another differentiator. Many enterprises prefer tools that have an open format (Mermaid’s syntax is plain text and can be stored in version control) to avoid vendor lock-in. Mermaid Chart benefits from Mermaid.js being an open standard of sorts; even if one doesn’t pay for Mermaid Chart, their diagrams are still just Mermaid code that could be rendered elsewhere. This openness builds trust and often encourages companies to adopt the paid platform knowing they won’t be stuck if they leave. It also means Mermaid Chart is often mentioned in the same breath as modern developer tools rather than just another SaaS app – giving it credibility among engineers.

- **Strategic Outlook:** In the near term (next 1–2 years), Mermaid Chart’s strategy is likely to **consolidate its position in the diagramming/tools market**: attracting teams that use the open-source Mermaid into the paid platform, continuing to add diagram types and polish the user experience, and building out enterprise must-haves (like SSO, admin controls, on-prem deployment). This will put it in closer competition with the Lucidcharts and Miros for departmental budgets. We can expect Mermaid Chart to emphasize its productivity gains (text definitions = faster editing, AI = less manual work, integration with code = single source of truth) to justify adoption over incumbents. Given its funding, Mermaid Chart will also invest in marketing to enterprise and developer communities to raise awareness that “Mermaid now has an enterprise-ready platform.” 

  Longer term, as indicated by the founders, Mermaid Chart aims to **expand into workflow and process management**. This could mean features that allow a diagram to not just be a static image but to trigger actions or integrate with other systems (imagine a flowchart that links to live data or can generate a runbook in ServiceNow). Such capabilities would move Mermaid Chart into overlapping territory with BPM software, IT documentation tools, or even low-code automation platforms. If successful, this strategy opens a much larger TAM (total addressable market) beyond just diagramming – potentially competing with tools like ServiceNow, Atlassian’s suite (for IT diagrams and runbooks), or process modeling tools like Signavio. It’s an ambitious play, essentially using diagrams as the “Trojan horse” into managing business processes. Notably, this vision is “quite a ways off” by Firestone’s own admission ([Mermaid Chart, a Markdown-like tool for creating diagrams, raises $7.5M | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/20/mermaid-chart-a-markdown-like-tool-for-creating-diagrams-raises-5-5m/#:~:text=easier,still%20quite%20a%20ways%20off)), meaning the company recognizes it must first win in the core diagramming/collaboration space before extending outward.

- **Challenges and Considerations:** Mermaid Chart’s market positioning comes with challenges. It must persuade organizations to adopt a new tool in a space where many already have subscriptions to alternatives. It has to maintain a balance between its **developer community roots** and the demands of paying enterprise customers – for instance, continuing to support the open-source Mermaid.js (which it has committed to do ([Announcements | Mermaid](https://mermaid.js.org/news/announcements.html#:~:text=Why%20It%20Matters%20for%20Mermaid,JS))) while developing proprietary features. The presence of tech giant investors (Microsoft’s M12) is interesting, considering Microsoft has Visio; this could signal potential partnerships (e.g., integration with Microsoft Teams or GitHub, beyond what’s already native) or simply a hedge by Microsoft to be involved in a promising new approach to productivity software. Mermaid Chart will also need to stay ahead on the innovation curve (AI features, etc.) because bigger players can potentially replicate some features over time.

In summary, **Mermaid Chart’s market position** is that of a **rising challenger** in the diagramming and collaboration domain, one that differentiates through its open-core origin, text+visual hybrid approach, and early mover advantage in AI-assisted diagramming. It straddles a niche of serving developers (where it faces little serious competition, given Mermaid’s dominance in that segment) and the broader enterprise diagramming space (where it takes on well-funded incumbents). If it executes well, Mermaid Chart could carve out a significant multi-disciplinary user base and gradually morph into a platform that not only draws diagrams, but also helps organizations document and automate their processes. Its strategic outlook is therefore twofold: **near-term**, become the go-to solution for any team that wants faster, smarter diagramming; **long-term**, leverage that position to enter the realm of dynamic workflows and enterprise knowledge management, positioning Mermaid Chart as an indispensable part of the modern distributed workplace.
